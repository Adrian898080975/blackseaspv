Payments and MSBs in Pakistan

Who is the AML/CFT supervisor for payments and MSBs in Pakistan?

The State Bank of Pakistan, for exchange companies.

For: Payment institutions, electronic money issuers, remittance providers and money service businesses.

Reporting: Suspicious transaction reports go to the Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU), Pakistan's FIU, through goAML. A reporting entity registers on goAML before it can file.

The MLRO seat: Every reporting entity appoints a compliance officer at a management level, under section 7G of the AML Act. Under the Virtual Assets Act, 2026, the money-laundering reporting officer is a Key Individual, and the definition covers a post held on a full-time, part-time, acting or outsourced basis. At least one Key Individual ordinarily resident in Pakistan must hold operational and decision-making authority.

Recent change: The Virtual Assets Act, 2026 (Act No. XIII of 2026) is dated 4 March 2026, came into force at once and established PVARA. Virtual asset service providers licensed under it are deemed financial institutions for the purposes of the AML Act, and they report suspicious transactions to the FMU.

The standard: Recommendation 14 requires providers of money or value transfer services to be licensed or registered and monitored for AML/CFT compliance, and to include their agents in their AML/CFT programme and monitor them. Under Recommendation 16, the required originator and beneficiary information goes with a payment or value transfer and stays with it through the payment chain. A provider that controls both sides of a transfer considers both before deciding whether to file a suspicious transaction report, and files it in any country affected.

What we build: the risk assessment by product, corridor and channel; agent due diligence and oversight; transaction monitoring rules set for your volumes; the payment transparency procedure; and sanctions screening of payments.
