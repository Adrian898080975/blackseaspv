United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, the supervisor depends on your business. The AML/CFT rules for businesses are the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017, and the duty to report suspicion comes from the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 and the Terrorism Act 2000.

Reporting: Suspicious activity reports (SARs) go to the UK Financial Intelligence Unit (UKFIU) in the National Crime Agency (NCA), through the NCA's SAR Portal. In the regulated sector, a person who knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting, money laundering or terrorist financing, from information that came to them in the course of that business, must disclose it to their nominated officer, or directly to the authorities, as soon as is practicable. Failing to do so is a criminal offence under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 and the Terrorism Act 2000.

The MLRO seat: A business appoints a nominated officer, who receives internal reports of suspicion, considers them and reports to the NCA where required. Where the size and nature of the business call for it, it also appoints an officer responsible for its compliance with the Regulations, who must be a member of the board or of senior management. It tells its supervisor who holds each role within 14 days of the appointment. A sole trader with no employees who does not act with anyone else need not appoint either.

Recent change: The Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2026 came into force on 30 June 2026. The enhanced due diligence the Regulations require for high-risk third countries now applies only to countries the FATF lists as subject to a call for action. Euro amounts in the Regulations became sterling amounts, selling an off-the-shelf firm became a trust or company service, and the due diligence rules for pooled client accounts changed.

Virtual assets: A cryptoasset exchange provider or custodian wallet provider must register with the FCA before it starts that business in the United Kingdom, and the FCA supervises it for AML/CFT. From 25 October 2027, under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026, a firm carrying on the new cryptoasset regulated activities will need the FCA's authorisation.
