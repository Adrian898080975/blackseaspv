Banks and financial institutions in Nigeria

Who is the AML/CFT supervisor for banks and financial institutions in Nigeria?

The Central Bank of Nigeria.

For: Retail, corporate and correspondent banks, and other financial institutions.

Reporting: Suspicious transaction reports go to the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit within twenty-four hours, mainly through goAML. The duty applies whether the transaction is complete or not.

The MLRO seat: Section 10 of the Act requires every financial institution and designated non-financial business or profession to designate compliance officers at management level. The regulations of the SEC and of SCUML, each for its own firms, require an officer with the competence, authority and independence to implement the compliance programme.

The standard: Under Recommendation 10, a financial institution identifies and verifies its customer, identifies the beneficial owner and takes reasonable measures to verify that person's identity, and keeps the relationship under ongoing due diligence. Under Recommendation 13, a bank that opens a cross-border correspondent relationship first understands the respondent's business, reputation and quality of supervision, obtains senior management approval, and keeps no relationship with a shell bank. Recommendation 18 requires internal controls with a compliance officer at management level, employee screening, ongoing training and an independent audit function, applied across the group, foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries included.

What we build: the business risk assessment; customer and beneficial owner due diligence; correspondent and nested relationship reviews; sanctions screening and transaction monitoring rules; and group-wide policies for branches and subsidiaries.
