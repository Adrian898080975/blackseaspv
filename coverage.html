How we work in your market

Senior practitioners lead each engagement, and law firms in the market take the local legal questions under their own engagement.

Most of the work is delivered remotely. Where a supervisor or the work itself needs someone on site, the costed plan says when and for how long.

Where a market allows an outside MLRO, we can provide one for an agreed period. Where the seat must be held by your own employee, or by a citizen or a resident of the market, your appointee holds it and we stand behind that person.

Bahrain

In Bahrain, the supervisor depends on your licence. The AML/CFT law is Decree-Law No. (4) of 2001, as amended.

Bahrain: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A bank, insurer, investment firm, money changer, trust service provider or capital market service providerThe Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB), under its Rulebook
A crypto-asset service providerThe CBB, by licence
A dealer in gold and jewellery, or an auditorThe Ministry of Industry and Commerce, under Decision No. (103) of 2021
A real estate brokerThe Real Estate Regulatory Authority

Reporting: Financial institutions licensed by the CBB file suspicious transaction reports through the Online STR system, to Bahrain's financial intelligence unit and to the CBB. Dealers in gold and jewellery, auditors and real estate brokers also report suspicious transactions to the financial intelligence unit.

The MLRO seat: At a conventional bank, the MLRO is a member of senior management, and the CBB must approve the appointment.

Banks: Conventional banks and Islamic banks each have their own volume of the CBB Rulebook. The Financial Crime module of each volume holds the detailed AML/CFT requirements, the MLRO's role and duties included.

Kuwait

In Kuwait, the supervisor depends on your licence. The AML/CFT law is Law No. (106) of 2013, as amended, and Ministerial Resolution No. (37) of 2013 issued its executive regulation.

Kuwait: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A bank, exchange company, finance company or e-payment companyThe Central Bank of Kuwait (CBK)
An investment company, securities or financial brokerage company, underwriter, asset manager, mutual fund or custodianThe Capital Markets Authority (CMA), under Module Sixteen of its Executive Bylaws
An insurance companyThe Insurance Regulatory Unit, under its AML/CFT rules for insurance, issued by Resolution No. (57) of 2023
A currency exchange organisation, real estate broker or agent, or dealer in precious metals and stonesThe Ministry of Commerce and Industry

Reporting: Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs) report to the Kuwait Financial Intelligence Unit (KwFIU) any transaction or attempted transaction, whatever its value, that they suspect, or have reasonable grounds to suspect, involves the proceeds of crime or funds linked to money laundering or terrorism financing. The law asks for the report without delay, and its executive regulation sets a limit of two working days after the suspicion forms. The KwFIU receives reports electronically through goAML.

The MLRO seat: Kuwaiti law calls the seat the compliance officer. Every financial institution and DNFBP designates one at senior management level, responsible for implementing the law, under Article 10 of Law No. (106) of 2013. A bank gives the CBK the details of its compliance officer and deputy, and informs the CBK of any change.

Virtual assets: Circulars of the CBK and the CMA of 17 July 2023 ban the use of virtual assets for payment or investment, and all virtual asset mining, in Kuwait, and no licence is issued for virtual asset services. Securities regulated by the CBK, and other securities and financial instruments regulated by the CMA, are outside the ban. The firms the circulars address must keep their customers informed of the risks of virtual asset dealings carried out outside Kuwait.

Oman

Oman: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor

Qatar

Qatar has two perimeters. Firms in the Qatar Financial Centre (QFC) answer to the Qatar Financial Centre Regulatory Authority (QFCRA), and firms onshore answer to national supervisors. Both are subject to Law No. (20) of 2019 on Combating Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing, amended by Law No. (18) of 2025, and to its Implementing Regulations, promulgated by Council of Ministers' Decision No. (41) of 2019.

Qatar: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
Onshore: a bank, exchange house, money or value transfer service provider, insurer or reinsurer, or finance or investment companyThe Qatar Central Bank (QCB), under its AML/CFT Instructions for financial institutions
Onshore: a financial brokerage firm or intermediary, the Qatar Stock Exchange or the Qatar Central Securities DepositoryThe Qatar Financial Markets Authority, under its AML/CFT Rules
Onshore: an auditor, a dealer in precious metals or precious stones, or a trust and company service providerThe Ministry of Commerce and Industry, under its AML/CFT Compliance Rules
Onshore: a lawyerThe Ministry of Justice, under its AML/CFT regulations for lawyers
In the QFC: a financial institution or a designated non-financial business or profession (DNFBP)The QFCRA, under its Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Rules 2019 (AML/CFTR)
In the QFC: a firm that conducts only general insurance business, or insurance mediation for general or non-investment insurance contractsThe QFCRA, under its AML/CFT (General Insurance) Rules 2019

Reporting: Financial institutions and DNFBPs in both perimeters report suspicious transactions to the Qatar Financial Information Unit (QFIU) promptly, whatever the value, attempted transactions included. The QFIU takes reports through its electronic system, ESTR, from firms with an electronic link to it, and by hand at its headquarters.

The MLRO seat: Every financial institution and DNFBP appoints a compliance officer at management level as part of its AML/CFT programme. A financial institution licensed by the QCB needs the QCB's approval to appoint or remove its MLRO and its Deputy MLRO.

Recent change: On 1 September 2024, the Qatar Financial Centre Authority (QFCA) and the QFCRA launched the QFC Digital Assets Framework 2024, for digital assets in the QFC and their tokenisation. The framework brought token service providers that are not financial institutions under the AML/CFTR. A token service provider in the QFC needs a programme written to those rules.

Funds and corporate service providers: In the QFC, a firm that undertakes portfolio management, or invests, administers or manages funds for others, is a financial institution under the AML/CFTR, and the QFCRA supervises it. A trust and company service provider in the QFC is a DNFBP with a QFCA licence and is supervised by the QFCRA; onshore, the Ministry of Commerce and Industry supervises it.

Saudi Arabia

In Saudi Arabia, the supervisor depends on your licence. Money laundering duties come from the Anti-Money Laundering Law, issued by Royal Decree No. (M/20) in 2017 and since amended, and from its implementing regulation. Terrorist financing falls under a separate law, issued by Royal Decree No. (M/21).

Saudi Arabia: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A bank, finance company, money changer or payment service providerThe Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)
A firm licensed to carry on securities businessThe Capital Market Authority
An insurance companyThe Insurance Authority
A dealer in precious metals and precious stonesThe Ministry of Commerce
A lawyer or law firmThe Ministry of Justice

Reporting: Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions, persons who provide legal or accounting services included, report to the General Directorate of Financial Intelligence immediately and directly when they suspect, or have reasonable grounds to suspect, that funds are proceeds of crime or linked to money laundering, attempts included. The duty applies whatever the amount, and the report follows the form the Directorate has approved.

The MLRO seat: The implementing regulation calls the seat the anti-money laundering compliance officer. Financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions appoint one at senior management level.

Recent change: An update of the implementing regulation adds the Insurance Authority to the supervisory authorities. A firm that reports a suspicion about a complex or large transaction must now also suspend its execution, under guidelines the Directorate issues with the supervisory authorities. The Directorate decides on such a report within two working days and may order the transaction suspended for up to seven working days from the date of the report.

Payments: Under the Law of Payments and Payment Services of 2021, no one may operate a payment system or provide payment services in Saudi Arabia without a licence from SAMA. Money changers hold a separate licence, and SAMA supervises them as well.

Virtual assets: In the DIFC, updated Crypto Token rules apply from 12 January 2026. The DFSA keeps no list of recognised tokens, and each firm assesses on a documented basis whether a token is suitable.

Jordan: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A bank, exchange company, microfinance company, or electronic payment and money transfer companyThe Central Bank of Jordan

Ghana

In Ghana, the supervisor depends on your licence or your business. The AML/CFT law is the Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2020 (Act 1044).

Ghana: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A bank, or a non-bank financial institution that the Bank of Ghana licensesThe Bank of Ghana
A foreign exchange bureau, payment service provider or dedicated electronic money issuerThe Bank of Ghana
A market operator that the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) licensesThe SEC
An insurance companyThe National Insurance Commission
An operator of games of chanceThe Gaming Commission of Ghana
A virtual asset service providerThe Bank of Ghana or the SEC, depending on the activity, by licence or registration under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025 (Act 1154)

Reporting: Suspicious transaction reports go to the Financial Intelligence Centre within twenty-four hours. Attempted transactions are reported too, whatever the amount.

Recent change: Under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025 (Act 1154), persons and entities that carry on virtual asset activities need a licence or registration from the Bank of Ghana or the SEC, depending on the activity.

Kenya

In Kenya, the Financial Reporting Centre (FRC) supervises all reporting institutions, and each supervisory body also supervises the firms it regulates. The AML/CFT law is the Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act, Cap. 59A (POCAMLA), with the Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Regulations, 2023 (Legal Notice 153 of 2023).

Kenya: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A commercial bank, microfinance bank, money remittance provider, payment service provider or foreign exchange bureauThe Central Bank of Kenya (CBK)
A capital markets firm, fund manager or collective investment schemeThe Capital Markets Authority (CMA)
An insurer, agent or broker in life or other investment-related insuranceThe Insurance Regulatory Authority
A retirement benefits schemeThe Retirement Benefits Authority
A deposit-taking Sacco societyThe Sacco Societies Regulatory Authority
A virtual asset exchange, trading platform, broker, investment adviser, manager or offering providerThe CMA, by licence under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025
A custodial wallet provider, virtual asset payment processor or stablecoin issuerThe CBK, by licence under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025
A real estate agentThe Estate Agents Registration Board
An accountantThe Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Kenya
An advocate, notary or other independent legal professionalThe Law Society of Kenya, as the self-regulatory body

Reporting: Suspicious transaction reports go to the FRC, Kenya's financial intelligence unit, through goAML, within two days after the suspicion arose. Every reporting institution must register with the FRC. The law lets advocates, notaries and other independent legal professionals report through the Law Society of Kenya, which is to set up reporting channels to the FRC.

The MLRO seat: Every reporting institution appoints a Money Laundering Reporting Officer at management level, under regulation 12 of the 2023 Regulations.

Recent change: The Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025, assented to on 15 October 2025, requires a licence for virtual asset services in or from Kenya and made providers reporting institutions under the POCAMLA. A provider needs its licence from the CMA or the CBK, by activity, and an AML/CFT programme that answers to that authority.

South Africa

In South Africa, the supervisor depends on your business. The AML/CFT law is the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 (Act 38 of 2001), the FIC Act, amended by the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act, 2022 (Act No. 22 of 2022). Schedule 2 of the FIC Act names a supervisory body for some kinds of accountable institution, and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) supervises the others.

South Africa: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
An authorised user of an exchange, a manager of a collective investment scheme, or a financial services provider that needs authorisation under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act, 2002 (FAIS Act) for advice or intermediary services on the investment of any financial productThe Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA)
A bank, a mutual or co-operative bank, or a life insurance business, reinsurance excludedThe South African Reserve Bank (SARB), with regard to the Prudential Authority
A dealer in foreign exchange, or a person who issues, sells or redeems travellers' cheques, money orders or similar instrumentsThe SARB, with regard to the Financial Surveillance Department
A money or value transfer providerThe SARB, with regard to the Prudential Authority and the Financial Surveillance Department, both named for it in Schedule 2
A crypto asset service providerThe FIC, for the activities in item 22 of Schedule 1, and the FSCA where the provider is also a financial services provider under the FAIS Act
A legal practitioner listed in Schedule 1, a trust or company service provider, an estate agent, a gambling business, a credit provider or a dealer in high-value goodsThe FIC

Reporting: Suspicious and unusual transaction reports go to the FIC under section 29 of the FIC Act, which applies to anyone who carries on, manages or is employed by a business. A person who knows or suspects, or ought reasonably to have known or suspected, one of the matters section 29 lists reports the grounds and the prescribed particulars within the prescribed period after the knowledge was acquired or the suspicion arose. Every accountable institution registers with the FIC on goAML, under the correct items of Schedule 1 and in some cases more than one.

The MLRO seat: Under section 42A of the FIC Act, an accountable institution that is a legal person has a compliance function and assigns a person with sufficient competence and seniority to ensure that the function is effective. The board of directors, or senior management where there is no board, must ensure compliance with the Act and with the Risk Management and Compliance Programme that section 42 requires.

Recent change: FIC Directive 9, in operation since 30 April 2025, applies the travel rule to crypto asset transfers by crypto asset service providers under items 12 and 22 of Schedule 1. The ordering provider must transmit to the recipient provider information on the originator and the beneficiary of a qualifying transfer, and both must include risk-based policies and procedures for transfers that involve unhosted wallets in their Risk Management and Compliance Programme. A programme written before it needs those procedures added.

Virtual assets: Since 19 December 2022, a business that exchanges or transfers crypto assets for a client, holds them in safekeeping or administers them, or provides financial services for an issuer's offer or sale of a crypto asset, is an accountable institution and registers with the FIC. In October 2022, the FSCA declared crypto assets a financial product under the FAIS Act, and it licenses crypto asset service providers for the advice and intermediary services they render on crypto assets.

Azerbaijan

In Azerbaijan, the supervisor depends on your licence or your profession. The AML/CFT law is Law No. 781-VIQ of 30 December 2022.

Azerbaijan: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A credit institution, pawnshop, finance lease provider or firm licensed for currency exchangeThe Central Bank of the Republic of Azerbaijan

An advocateThe Bar Association of the Republic of Azerbaijan

Recent change: Law No. 240-VIIQD of 8 July 2025 made organisers and operators of casino games obliged persons under Law No. 781-VIQ.

Real estate and DNFBPs

For: Real estate agents and developers, lawyers, notaries, accountants and the other designated non-financial businesses and professions.

The standard: Under Recommendation 22, real estate agents apply customer due diligence to both purchaser and vendor when they act for a client in buying or selling real estate. Lawyers, notaries, other independent legal professionals and accountants apply it in five activities for a client, from buying and selling real estate and managing client money or assets to creating legal persons and buying and selling businesses, and report suspicious transactions when they engage in a financial transaction in those activities (Recommendation 23). They are not required to report information obtained under professional secrecy or legal professional privilege, and dissuading a client from illegal activity does not amount to tipping-off.

Defence and dual-use

For: Defence firms, makers and traders of dual-use goods, and firms in export-sensitive trade.

The standard: Under Recommendations 6 and 7, countries require every natural and legal person to freeze, without delay and without prior notice, the funds or other assets of persons and entities designated under the UN Security Council resolutions, those on the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and its financing included. Since October 2020, Recommendation 1 has required financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions to identify, assess and mitigate their proliferation financing risk, which means strictly the potential breach, non-implementation or evasion of those sanctions. UN Security Council resolution 1540 (2004) separately requires every State to establish domestic controls against the proliferation of nuclear, chemical or biological weapons and their means of delivery, national export and trans-shipment controls included, and the Participating States of the Wassenaar Arrangement implement export controls through national legislation.

What we build: sanctions screening of customers, end users and counterparties; end-use and diversion checks; the proliferation financing risk assessment; and the link between your export control function and your financial crime controls.
