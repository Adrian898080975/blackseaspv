02Your obligation here

In Nigeria, payments and money-services activity answers to the Central Bank, with financial-crime supervision running through two further bodies. The obligation behind the licence is consistent in shape: the current framework requires a named compliance officer, a written AML/CFT programme, a documented risk assessment, risk-based customer due diligence, ongoing sanctions and PEP screening, transaction monitoring, and suspicious-transaction reporting to the NFIU.

Licensing & prudential
CBN The Central Bank of Nigeria licenses and supervises payment service providers and money-services operators. Supervised-institutions directory: cbn.gov.ng/Supervision/fi.asp
AML/CFT registration
SCUML The Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering registers and supervises the AML side for the relevant operators. Site: scuml.efcc.gov.ng
Financial-intelligence reporting
NFIU The Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit receives suspicious-transaction and threshold reporting. Site: nfiu.gov.ng
Any specific CBN requirement, licence-category or capital threshold, SCUML registration timeline or reporting deadline is confirmed against the CBN or SCUML source before it is stated. The sentence stays generic if unconfirmed.

04Why it is urgent now
Standing exposure
Payments and money services in Nigeria are fully supervised today. The CBN licenses the activity, SCUML registers the AML side, and the NFIU receives the reports, so an operator without a defensible AML/CFT programme is exposed at the next examination, not at some future deadline. Cross-border money movement puts a payments operator squarely in the sanctions-screening line of sight, which is where thin programmes fail first.
