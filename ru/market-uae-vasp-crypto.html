02Your obligation here

The UAE runs virtual-asset supervision through four distinct authorities, and which one you answer to depends on where you sit. Whichever regime you fall under, the obligation behind the licence is the same in shape: a named, fit-and-proper compliance officer, a written AML/CFT programme, a documented risk assessment, risk-based CDD, transaction monitoring, sanctions screening, and suspicious-transaction reporting to the UAE FIU through goAML.

Dubai mainland
VARA The Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority licenses and supervises VASPs (outside DIFC). Public register: vara.ae/en/licenses-and-register/public-register
Abu Dhabi (ADGM)
ADGM FSRA The Financial Services Regulatory Authority authorises virtual-asset activity in the ADGM free zone. Register: adgm.com/public-registers/fsra
Dubai (DIFC)
DIFC DFSA The Dubai Financial Services Authority runs the DIFC crypto-token regime. Register: dfsa.ae/public-register/firms
Federal securities
CMA The Capital Market Authority (formerly SCA) sits over the wider capital-market side. Site: uaecma.gov.ae
Any specific VARA, ADGM FSRA or DIFC DFSA rulebook threshold, capital requirement, filing deadline or transition date is confirmed against that regulator's own rulebook before it is stated. The sentence stays generic if unconfirmed.

04Why it is urgent now
Standing exposure
Virtual assets in the UAE are already fully supervised. VARA, ADGM FSRA and DIFC DFSA license the activity today, and each maintains a public register, so an operator without a defensible AML/CFT programme is exposed at the next inspection, not at some future deadline. The pressure is standing, not seasonal.
