Cayman Islands

In the Cayman Islands, the supervisor depends on your business. The AML/CFT rules for businesses are set out in the Anti-Money Laundering Regulations (AMLRs), made under the Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA), and they apply to every business that carries on relevant financial business.

Cayman Islands: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A bank or trust company, an insurer, an investment fund or another financial services provider regulated by the Cayman Islands Monetary AuthorityCayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA)
A virtual asset service providerCIMA
A real estate agent or broker, or a dealer in precious metals or precious stonesDepartment of Commerce and Investment (DCI)
An accounting firmCayman Islands Institute of Professional Accountants (CIIPA)
A law firm or sole practitionerLegal Services Supervisory Authority (LSSA)

Reporting: Suspicious activity reports (SARs) go to the Financial Reporting Authority (FRA), the Cayman Islands' financial intelligence unit. Since 2 January 2025, filing a SAR no longer gives a defence on its own: to have a defence against money laundering, a business must also obtain the FRA's consent before it goes ahead. If the FRA does not respond within seven working days, consent is deemed given; if it refuses, a moratorium of 30 calendar days follows. Reports under targeted financial sanctions also go to the FRA.

The MLRO seat: A financial services provider regulated by CIMA must designate an AML Compliance Officer (AMLCO), a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) and a Deputy MLRO, each a natural person at management level. The MLRO receives suspicious activity reports from staff and decides whether to report to the FRA; the Deputy MLRO acts in the MLRO's absence. Designated non-financial businesses and professions, such as real estate agents, accounting firms and law firms, give their supervisor the names of their AML officers when they register.

Recent change: Since 18 September 2026, two CIMA rules have been in force for the financial services providers it regulates: one on effective compliance programmes against money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing, and one on compliance with financial sanctions and targeted financial sanctions. They largely restate existing obligations as rules that CIMA can enforce directly, and a breach can lead to fines or other enforcement action.

Virtual assets: Since 1 April 2025, a business that provides virtual asset custody services or operates a virtual asset trading platform needs a licence from CIMA. Other virtual asset services generally require registration.
