Funds and corporate service providers in Switzerland

Who is the AML/CFT supervisor for funds and corporate service providers in Switzerland?

The Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority, for institutions under the Collective Investment Schemes Act, and indirectly, through a supervisory organisation, for portfolio managers and trustees.

For: Fund managers, fund administrators, and trust and company service providers.

Reporting: Suspicious activity reports go to the Money Laundering Reporting Office Switzerland (MROS) at the Federal Office of Police (fedpol), through MROS's own information system. A financial intermediary must report immediately when it knows or has reasonable grounds to suspect that assets in a business relationship are linked to money laundering, terrorist financing or a criminal or terrorist organisation, or are the proceeds of a felony or an aggravated tax misdemeanour. It must also report when it breaks off talks on a new business relationship because of such a suspicion. Since 1 October 2026, advisers covered by the Act must report on the same grounds.

The MLRO seat: A financial intermediary must take the organisational measures its business needs to prevent money laundering and terrorist financing, including adequate training for its staff. A business in the first two rows of the table must also have its own anti-money laundering unit, which oversees that training. A member of a self-regulatory organisation follows the organisation's regulations, which set out its anti-money laundering duties in detail, and the organisation checks that it complies.

Recent change: Amendments to the Anti-Money Laundering Act came into force on 1 October 2026. Advisers who, on a professional basis, assist with financial transactions linked to the legal acts the Act lists, such as buying or selling real estate, now have due diligence duties, must report suspicions to MROS and must join a self-regulatory organisation.

The standard: The FATF defines portfolio management, and investing, administering or managing funds for others, as financial institution activities. Trust and company service providers apply customer due diligence and report suspicious transactions when they act for a client as formation agent, director, secretary, partner, trustee of an express trust or nominee shareholder, arrange for another person to act so, or provide a registered office (Recommendations 22 and 23). Recommendations 24 and 25 require adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership information on legal persons and on express trusts, which competent authorities can obtain in a timely manner.

What we build: due diligence on investors and beneficial owners through every layer of a structure; source of wealth checks; procedures for formation, director, nominee and trustee services; and the beneficial ownership records a supervisor can ask for.
