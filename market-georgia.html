Georgia

In Georgia, the supervisor depends on your licence or your profession. The AML/CFT law is the Law of Georgia on Facilitating the Prevention of Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism, of 30 October 2019.

Georgia: supervisor by licence
If you areYour supervisor
A commercial bank, micro bank, microfinance organisation, credit union, loan issuing entity or currency exchange bureauThe National Bank of Georgia (NBG)
A payment service providerThe NBG
A brokerage company, securities registrar, asset management company, investment fund without an assigned asset management company, or the central depositoryThe NBG
A virtual asset service providerThe NBG, by registration under the rule approved by Order No. 94/04 of the NBG President of 13 June 2023
An insurance organisation or pension companyThe Insurance State Supervision Service of Georgia
A leasing company, an organiser of lotteries, gambling or other commercial games, or a trader of precious stones and metalsThe Ministry of Finance of Georgia
A certified accountant, an accountant providing professional services, an accounting firm, an auditor or an audit firmThe Service for Accounting, Reporting and Auditing Supervision, under the Ministry of Finance
A notaryThe Ministry of Justice of Georgia
A lawyer or law firmThe Georgian Bar Association

Reporting: Suspicious transaction reports go to the Financial Monitoring Service of Georgia (FMS) through its online system on the day a reasonable suspicion forms, whatever the amount, attempts included. Each firm needs a software system, proportionate to its business and risks, to identify suspicious transactions and report them. A firm that starts operating registers with the FMS through its web portal within 10 business days of receiving its licence, registration or permission.

The MLRO seat: Georgian law puts the person who runs the compliance system, or the head of the unit that runs it, at senior management level. That person answers to a member of the governing body, or a person with managing authority, whom the firm makes responsible for the system's effectiveness. The compliance officer decides independently whether to file a report, and the firm protects the officer against threats, discriminatory treatment and other unlawful influence.

Recent change: Law No. 1460 of 1 April 2026 makes factoring companies and factoring platforms obliged entities supervised by the NBG, from 1 January 2027. From that date, the reporting and compliance duties above apply to them.
